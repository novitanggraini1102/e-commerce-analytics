# Executive Summary

**Home-goods e-commerce, 2024-2025 - 26 October 2026**

**Bottom line:** The business is growing quickly (21.2% revenue growth in 2025, $1,881,974 over two years, 49.2% gross margin), but growth depends on constant customer acquisition, and four areas need attention.

| What we found | Number | What to do |
|---|---|---|
| **Furniture is returned far more than anything else** | 12.1% of lines vs 4.6% elsewhere; 33% of all returned value from 17% of revenue | Capture return reasons; audit the 5 worst products |
| **Paid social customers rarely buy** | 23% of customers, 13% of revenue; 44% ever buy vs 59%-68% in main channels | Get spend data; test budget shift to referral/organic |
| **Growth is acquisition-led** | Active buyers +92%, revenue per buyer -37%; 50% of 2024 buyers returned in 2025 | Track retention by cohort |
| **Holiday discounts cost margin; payoff unproven** | HOLIDAY20 orders: 38% margin vs 50%; $32,001 given | Holdout test before next November |

**Also:** 41.5% of registered customers never ordered; 81 heavy buyers (4%) generate 26% of revenue.

**Context:** US e-commerce grew 5.4% in 2025 (Census), so the business outpaces the market, from a small base. Online return rates across US retail average about 19% (NRF), so furniture's 12% is high *internally*, not against the industry.

**Be careful with:** the apparent margin gain (47.8% to 50.4%) is likely an artifact of static product costs; causes of returns and the profitability of channels cannot be determined without return reasons and marketing spend.
